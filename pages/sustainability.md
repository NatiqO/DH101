# Sustainability & Ethics

## My Research

This project showed me that AI has a much bigger environmental impact than I originally thought. It connects to the idea of extractive AI, where the benefits of technology are concentrated, but the environmental costs are spread across ecosystems and communities. It also relates to the idea of the “tragedy of the commons,” where widespread use of AI for individual convenience can lead to larger collective consequences.

Training large AI models requires a significant amount of energy and can produce high levels of carbon emissions. For example, training models like GPT-3 has been estimated to produce hundreds of tons of CO₂. Even everyday AI use adds up over time, especially as more people rely on these systems. This shows that AI is not just digital, but depends on physical infrastructure like data centers that consume energy and resources.

## Personal Reflection

Before this project, I did not think much about the environmental impact of AI. After estimating my own usage, I realized that it can add up to a noticeable amount of CO₂ over time. This made me more aware of how often I use AI and the impact that comes with it.

Going forward, I plan to use AI more intentionally rather than automatically. I would not stop using it completely because it is useful, but I think it is important to be more mindful of when and how I use it.

## Systemic vs. Individual Responsibility

Individuals play a role through how they use AI tools, but most of the responsibility falls on companies. They decide how AI systems are built, how much energy they use, and how transparent they are about their environmental impact.

Real change will likely come from improving efficiency, using more sustainable energy sources, and increasing transparency around AI systems. While individual choices matter, long-term impact depends more on systemic changes.
