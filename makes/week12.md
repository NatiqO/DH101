### Research Documentation

For this project, I used a mix of sources to understand the environmental impact of artificial intelligence, especially its carbon footprint. The Stanford University School of Engineering article helped explain how AI systems rely on data centers and powerful computers that use large amounts of electricity. The MIT Technology Review article provided more detail about how much energy AI actually consumes and how its impact is often underestimated or overlooked. I also used Wikipedia for general background information, which helped me understand the scale of online platforms and how widely AI is used. Also used, the ExxonMobil page on carbon capture to get data to better understand how carbon emissions are measured, and how much AI is producing in comparison to aviation industries.

The hardest data to find was exact numbers for AI emissions. While I used the estimates you provided and found estimates, such as training a model like GPT-3 producing around 552 tons of CO₂, it was difficult to find consistent or officially confirmed values. Different sources sometimes gave slightly different numbers, and there is no single standard measurement. It was also challenging to find reliable data for everyday AI use, since most research focuses on large-scale training rather than individual usage. This made it harder to calculate personal impact with complete accuracy.

There are also several uncertainties in the data I used. Most of the numbers are estimates rather than exact measurements because companies do not always share full details about their energy use. Emissions can also vary depending on factors like the type of energy being used, the efficiency of data centers, and how often systems are used. Because of this, the values in this project should be understood as approximations. Even with these uncertainties, the research still clearly shows that AI has a real and often hidden environmental impact that is important to consider.

### Artist Statement

The environmental cost of AI that concerns me the most is its carbon footprint. Before this project, I didn’t realize how much energy it actually takes to train and run AI systems. Learning that training a single model can produce emissions equal to hundreds of flights or multiple households made the impact feel much more serious and real. It changed how I see AI, not just as a digital tool, but as something that has a real physical impact on the environment.

I dont think AI can become fully sustainable. Even if companies switch to renewable energy, AI still depends on constant growth, more data, and more powerful systems. That means more data centers, more energy use, and more resource extraction. And then we also have to think of the competition between companies and countries. Each of them are trying to have the stronger computing power which causes them to focus more on getting faster and better system using up more resources. Also The idea of “the cloud” hides the fact that AI is built on physical infrastructure that will always have some environmental cost. Because of this, alot of people are not going to be aware and will always be trade-offs between the benefits of AI and its impact on the environment.

When it comes to responsibility, both individuals and companies play a role, but companies have more control overall. They decide how these systems are built and how much they grow. At the same time, people still contribute based on how often they use it. For me, that just means being a little more aware and not using it when I don’t really need to. This project made me realize that even though it’s useful, it’s not harmless, and there are real environmental costs behind it.
