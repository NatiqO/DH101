## Network Analysis
The network shows the relationships between apps, companies, devices, and data in my personal digital ecosystem. The most important  nodes are Google, Apple, and Meta because they connect to many other elements throughout the entire network. Google especially, since it owns multiple services like YouTube, Gmail, and Google Search, and it collects large amounts of data. Then you also have Apple which is also another important node because it controls the iPhone and Safari, which many apps depend on.
The network forms several connected groups. One group includes social media apps like TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, and WhatsApp, which are all focused on communication and content sharing. Another group includes Google services like YouTube, Gmail, Google Search, and Google Maps, which are tied together through one company and a shared data system. There is also a data-focused group made up of location data, messages, search history, viewing habits, and purchase history. These groups show how different parts of my digital life are organized and separate but still connected through the dominant companies that manage and control them.
What surprised me most is how many connections lead back to a small number of companies, which are Google, Microsoft, Apple, etc. Even though the apps seem separate at first, they are mostly controlled by the same corporations when you trace the connections. What is missing from this network is also important. It does not show hidden algorithms, data brokers, or the full extent of data sharing behind the scenes. These invisible systems play a major role in shaping how these platforms function and how users experience them.
Another important thing this network shows is how little control the user actually has. Even though this is my personal digital ecosystem, I’m not really at the center of it. Instead, the companies and platforms have most of the power because they control how data is collected and used. This shows that even though people use these apps every day, the real control comes from the systems behind them. Seeing the network like this changes how I think about it, from just a bunch of apps to a bigger system that controls how everything connects and works.

## Artist Statement
Like I mentioned earlier, when you look around and mess with the network, you realize how the power in digital and AI systems is concentrated in a small number of companies. Companies like Google, Apple, Meta, and ByteDance are highly visible because they control a lot of  apps, devices, and data flows. These companies appear at the center of the network, showing how much influence they have over how information moves and how users interact with technology. They are connected to many different apps and data types, which makes them more powerful compared to other parts of the network.
When it comes to visibility and invisibility, the companies and major platforms are the most visible parts of the network. They stand out because they have the most connections and are easy to recognize. However, what remains invisible are the systems working behind the scenes, such as algorithms, background tracking, and data sharing between platforms. These hidden processes play a huge role in shaping what users see and how their data is used, even though they are not clearly shown in the network. The user is also somewhat invisible in terms of power, since they mainly appear as a source of data rather than someone with control over how that data is handled.
If the network were structured differently, it could tell a different story. For example, if it focused more on data brokers, advertisers, or algorithms, it would show how much tracking and influence actually happens behind the scenes. This would make the hidden parts of the system more visible. It shows that networks don’t just display information, they also affect how we understand power based on what is included and what is excluded.
