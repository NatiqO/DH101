### Design Statement

My game takes place in Emberfall, a city where power determines status and survival. I made
this story with some ideas from an anime I recently completed watching. The main idea behind the
game is to explore how systems control who gets power and who doesn’t, similar to how AI systems
today can decide opportunities, visibility, and influence. The player starts as someone without
abilities and must navigate different paths depending on where they come from and what choices
they make.

The most important choices in the game involve whether the player decides to conform to the
system, survive quietly, or challenge it. For example, players can choose to either investigate the core
that gives people their abilities, join groups that resist the system, or choose to remain invisible.

These choices matter because in my game each of the choices lead to different endings that reflect
different relationships to power—survival, rebellion, escape, or complicity.

The structure of my game supports the theme through how each path branches out. Players who
take risks uncover more information and gain more control, while those who avoid danger have
fewer options and less influence over the outcome. This reflects procedural rhetoric, where the rules
of the game communicate an argument, that systems of power are not neutral, and that access to
power is often controlled rather than earned. By limiting or expanding choices based on decisions,
the game shows how agency can be restricted by larger systems, similar to how AI can shape
outcomes in real life.

Another important part of my game is how where you start affects what you can do. Each area in
Emberfall gives you different options, showing that not everyone has the same chances. This
connects to AI in real life, where some people benefit more than others. It shows that your choices
matter, but the system around you also plays a big role in what’s possible.

### Artist Statement

Through this project, I learned that games can be more than just stories but systems that shape how
players experience meaning. Unlike a traditional story, where everything is fixed, interactive
narratives require thinking about how choices affect outcomes and what those choices say about the
world being created. It also made me think about things other than games, things like how anime or
movies also reflect more of things going on in current society.

One of the most important things I realized is that the structure of a game can communicate ideas
just as much as the writing itself. In my game, the different paths reflect how power operates in
Emberfall. Some choices like what part of emberfall you are from either North, South, West, or East
lead to more control and knowledge, while others limit the player’s options. This helped me
understand the idea of procedural rhetoric which basically means influencing how people think or
act through the rules and structure of a system, instead of just using words or images, like how video
games guide players through their choices.

Working with AI was helpful, but it also showed me its limits. AI was useful for troubleshooting
and learning how to structure the game, especially with inky’s confusing syntax. And while I
basically came up with the main idea and the creative aspect of the story, deciding what the game
was about, what choices mattered, and how the player would experience the world, Ai helped
through the process, especially with giving options on what each path could potentially lead to.

If I were to improve this project, I would make the story a little longer and add more branching
paths, more options and deeper consequences for each decision.
