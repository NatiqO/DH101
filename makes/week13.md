### Context Statement

This artifact depicts Columbus, Ohio in the year 2042, almost ten years after the DC-17
data center was shut down and transformed into The Green Loop. In this future, the
environmental cost of artificial intelligence became too large and visible for the public to ignore.
Throughout the late 2020s and early 2030s, AI systems expanded rapidly, requiring more data
centers, more electricity, more water for cooling, and more land. At first, this growth was
presented as progress. AI was marketed as something in the cloud, efficient, intelligent, and
necessary for the future. However, some communities living near data centers began to
experience the hidden costs of AI more directly, rising energy demand, pressure on local water
supplies, increased heat, and limited say in how these facilities were built, causing the worry of
these people to increase.

You are probably wondering what led to this. The key turning point in this present was
the Clean Compute Act of 2032, which was an act that essentially forced companies to disclose
the environmental impact of their AI infrastructure and created limits on data centers in regions
that were already facing high stress due to AI. Therefore, DC-17 was shut down under this act
and later transferred into community ownership. Instead of simply demolishing it, residents
turned it into a public space focused on food justice, climate education, youth programs, and AI
literacy.

This future exposes a more complicated relationship between humans and AI. Although
AI is not treated as evil, it is also no longer treated as neutral or automatically beneficial. The
artifact shows that AI depends on physical systems: buildings, water, energy, workers, land, and
communities. It challenges the idea that technology exists “in the cloud,” separate from
real-world consequences. At the same time, my artifact is not a complete rejection of AI. The AI
literacy programs in The Green Loop can show AI still exists in the future and remains fairly
relevant. However, its role has changed. It is no longer treated as an unlimited, always-on
resource. Instead, it is something that must be understood, managed, and, at times, limited in
order to align with environmental and social priorities.

The image also shows an alternative future where communities are not passive users of
technology. They become able to make decisions themselves. Instead of asking only what AI can
do, this future asks who it benefits, who it harms, and what society is willing to sacrifice for
convenience and innovation. DC-17 represents the people's response to AI’s unintended
consequences. It suggests that the future of AI will not be defined solely by innovation and
progress, but by how we as a society choose to confront the costs that come with it.

### Critical Reflection

This future can be considered desirable and appealing on the surface depending on the
way you view it, but it is built on the aftermath of damage rather than prevention. The green loop
shows one space where the community has taken back control and turned it into something
useful and sustainable. Instead of it being one of the countless data centers that are mainly
focused on AI systems, it became a place that supports people through food, education, and a
space, which can make it feel like a positive outcome.

At the same time the existence of this space also implies that things had to go wrong first.
The transformation of DC-17 didn’t happen because companies suddenly decided to change, it
happened because the environmental and social costs became too big to ignore. This says that in
the present, we are not being proactive about AI’s impact. We are letting it expand first and only
thinking about consequences later. The future in this artifact is not one where the harm is
avoided, but rather one where the harm forces people to respond.

Which brings up the issue with how AI is being spoken about right now. AI is framed as
essential, progress, faster, smarter, and more efficient, but all these ignore what it truly takes to
make all of that possible. Using the “cloud” again as an example makes everything feel invisible,
when in reality it depends on real infrastructure that uses energy, water, and land that causes real
damage to our environment. My artifact challenges that illusion by making the infrastructure
visible and showing a possible/very likely future.

This future is neither inevitable nor entirely avoidable, it exists as one of the possible
outcomes. Especially if environmental concerns keep being ignored and only addressed after
they reach a crisis level. At the same time, this future introduces new trade-offs rather than
eliminating them. In order to attempt to prevent this future, Limiting data centers and regulating
AI infrastructure could slow technological developments, and reduce the damage and risks on the
environment. Data centers are becoming very populous, that there are even rumors of our school
Denison trying to get rid of the homestead to have its own data center.

This artifact clearly shows that the point is not that AI should disappear. The point is that
AI should not be allowed to grow endlessly without accountability. It's about how we choose to
manage AI and presents the question whether we will keep prioritizing AI expansion, or if we
are willing to rethink the system before the consequences force us to.
