### Research Documentation

For this project, I focused on data labeling as a form of hidden labor behind AI systems. I
mostly used AI to find most of my information but I also read the Ghost Work by Mary Gray and
Siddharth Suri, along with some articles I found online about the scale of AI. Although I’m
focusing on data labeling I also found some work related to gig work and AI labor conditions for
content moderation, seeing how the workers are paid and treated.

One of the hardest things to find was information about wages and exactly how much these gig
workers were actually being paid. Since all of the work is being done online and they were all
being hired not as employees but as outside workers, the pay varied a lot, it was also really hard
to find any sources from the workers themselves, I expected to be able to find articles or online
posts from the gig workers themselves but it was almost impossible to find anything, which
explain invisibility part of the AI systems.

A LOT of things surprised me while researching this topic, with the first being that the workers
almost never actually see the bigger picture or what they are actually contributing to since most
of the work is broken into small pieces, like labeling one image or transcribing a few seconds of
audio. The second thing that surprised me was how much this benefited the big companies. With
the work being spread across thousands of people, the companies are able to avoid the
responsibilities for wages, working conditions, and benefits, treating the workers as contractors
and not employees. Lastly, is how normalized this has become, most people don't even question
how AI is made, who is involved in the making of AI, or assume its all machines, showing the
“ghost work” or “crowdwork” phenomenon.

### Design Statement

One of the main reasons I chose to make an infographic was because I had never made one
before. I had the opportunity to create zines in a class I took last semester, so I wanted to try
something new and challenge myself with something new. I also felt that an infographic fits this
topic well because it allows people to understand information quickly just by looking at it. It
breaks things down into clear sections and keeps everything simple and readable instead of
overwhelming the viewer with long paragraphs.

I made my infographic to be easy to read to better match my audience, which is mainly people
online like reel watchers, TikTok doom scrollers, and everyday users of AI. Most people today
after being on their phones for so long don’t want to spend a lot of time reading long texts, so the
infographic is quick but still meaningful. A lot of people's attention span including mine are
definitely getting shorter so the goal of the infographic was to fit within shorter attention spans
while still keeping the message.

What I want people to take away from this is that AI is not as independent as it might look.
There are real people behind it doing repetitive and often low-paid work that most users never
see or even themselves never see. I want viewers to feel surprised and more aware of this hidden
labor after seeing it. It can make them think more critically about the technology they use every
day and question what is hidden behind it.
