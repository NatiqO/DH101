### Process (Prompt Documentation)

I used an AI image generator to create a series of tornado images in an active city environment.
First thing I did was open all the AI image generators I was going to use, which were ChatGPT,
Midjourney, Pollo AI, and ImagineArt. Most of the generators had a daily limit or a ticket/token
so I had to make sure I didn't accidentally waste them (which ended up happening). I started with
a base prompt describing “a large tornado with storm clouds and wind”. From there, I created
multiple variations by changing elements such as lighting, environment details, and level of
destruction using key words like “add another tornado”, “make them clash”, “put it in between
an active city”, “add fire and lighting”.

The main thing I controlled was the prompt. I kept the main idea the same, but kept changing
small little details each time to create variations of the image. For example, like I mentioned
earlier I added things like more fire, stronger lightning, or extra destruction. I also changed the
setting slightly, like putting the tornado in different parts of the city or adjusting the perspective
from wide views to close-up scenes.

In total, I generated around 30 images over 3 days. I tried different variations like adding more
fire, increasing the lightning, and changing the camera angle from wide shots to close-up views.
A lot of the images didn’t turn out how I expected. Some looked the exact same, others didn’t
have enough detail, and some were very unrealistic. Some images also didn’t have a clear focus,
which made them feel messy or confusing. The best images were the ones that looked realistic,
had strong detail, and clearly showed what was happening without feeling too cluttered, unlike
the ones I ended up rejecting because of lack of detail or creativity.

### Artist Statement

This project made me think about what creativity really means when AI can generate images
endlessly. The AI is able to create a lot of different versions of the same idea very quickly, but
that doesn’t mean all of them are going to end up good or even meaningful. Which makes
creativity in this case not just about generating images, but about making choices and deciding
what actually works.

The human part of my process came from how I controlled the prompts and how I selected the
final images. I chose and decided what the main idea would be, what details to change, and how
far to push each variation of the images. Even though the AI created the images, I was the one
guiding it and choosing which results to keep. I also had to decide and think of how to frame and
write my prompt in response to how the AI was responding to the prompt.

Deciding what was good wasn't that hard, I decided what was “good” based on a few things.
First, I looked for images that felt realistic and detailed. I also chose ones where the tornado and
destruction were clear and easy to understand, instead of messy or confusing. Another important
factor in my decisions was how intense and dramatic the image felt, since that matched the idea I
was going for. Most of the images I didn't choose were either too repetitive or didn’t stand out
enough to really give that impression I was going for.
