# Reflection 4: Is AI a collaborator, tool, or plagiarist in storytelling?

## Response

I see AI as both a tool and a collaborator, depending on how it is used. It works as a tool because it helps generate ideas, drafts, or visuals when I need a starting point. At the same time, it feels like a collaborator because it can suggest directions or ideas I would not have thought of on my own, which can push my thinking further.

This idea connects to *Critical Making in the Age of AI*, which explains that working with digital tools is not just about using them, but about actively shaping and engaging with what they produce. In storytelling, AI becomes more than just a tool when it starts influencing the direction of the work. However, it is still not a true collaborator in the same way a human is, because it does not have intention or understanding. It is generating outputs based on patterns rather than contributing meaning.

The idea of AI as a plagiarist also comes up, since it is trained on large amounts of existing human work. Because of that, its outputs can sometimes feel like a remix of things that already exist rather than something completely new. This raises questions about originality and ownership, especially if people rely on AI without making changes.

## Personal Reflection

Personally, I have found AI most useful when I treat it as a starting point instead of a final answer. It helps me get ideas quickly, but I still have to decide what to keep and what to change. When I rely on it too much, the work starts to feel less original. This shows that AI can act like a collaborator, but only if I stay in control of the process.

## Source

> Johnson, Emily K., and Anastasia Salter. *Critical Making in the Age of AI*
