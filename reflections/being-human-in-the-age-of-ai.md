# Final Reflection: What does it mean to be human in the age of AI?

## Response

At the start of this course, I saw AI mainly as a helpful tool that could make tasks easier and faster. Over time, my understanding changed. I now see AI as something that reflects human values, decisions, and systems rather than something separate from us. This shifted how I think about the debate between humanity and AI. It is not really about competition or replacement, but about understanding what remains uniquely human as machines take on more roles.

One of the biggest ideas that stood out to me is that AI does not actually understand meaning. As explained in *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots*, AI systems generate outputs based on patterns in data rather than true comprehension :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}. This made me realize that even when AI sounds human, it is not thinking, feeling, or interpreting the world in the same way. Because of this, human meaning comes from things AI cannot replicate, such as lived experience, emotion, intention, and the ability to make ethical decisions.

I also learned that AI is not neutral. The systems we build are shaped by the data they are trained on and the people who design them. This means AI can reinforce existing inequalities, especially when certain groups are underrepresented or misrepresented. Looking at the global side of AI, it also became clear that power, labor, and environmental costs are not distributed equally. The benefits are often concentrated in a few places, while the work and impact are spread across many others.

Another important takeaway for me was how AI changes creativity and authorship. Instead of creating everything from scratch, people now guide and shape what AI produces. This made me realize that creativity is still human, but it shows up differently through choices, interpretation, and intention. Even when AI generates content, the meaning behind it still depends on the person using it.

At the same time, learning about the environmental impact of AI made me question its long term sustainability. AI systems require large amounts of energy and resources, which makes it important to think about whether the benefits always justify the cost. This added another layer to how I think about responsibility in using AI.

## Personal Reflection

Overall, this course changed how I see both AI and myself. I used to focus mostly on what AI can do, but now I think more about how and why it is used. Being human in the age of AI is not about competing with machines, but about staying aware, making intentional choices, and taking responsibility for the systems we create. It means questioning outputs, thinking critically, and making sure our values are reflected in how AI is used.

In the end, the future of AI is not something that just happens to us. It is shaped by human decisions. That means being human in the age of AI is about accountability, awareness, and the ability to decide what kind of world we want to create with these tools.
