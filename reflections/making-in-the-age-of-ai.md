# Reflection 1: What counts as “making” in the age of AI?

## Response

In the age of AI, making is no longer just about physically creating something, but about guiding, shaping, and thinking through the process of creation. Instead of doing everything by hand, making now includes prompting AI, editing outputs, and deciding what to keep or change. This shifts the role of the creator from someone who only produces to someone who actively directs and interprets the work.

This idea connects to *Critical Making in the Age of AI*, which emphasizes that the value of making comes from the process, not just the final product :contentReference. Making is meant to help people think critically about technology and understand the systems they are using. In the context of AI, this means that simply generating something is not enough. What matters is how you engage with the output and what decisions you make along the way.

AI makes creation faster and more accessible, but it can also reduce hands on understanding if people rely on it too much. If someone just accepts what AI produces without questioning it, they miss the deeper thinking that comes from the process. Making is still important because it helps people stay connected to their work and understand how it was created.

## Personal Reflection

Personally, I have noticed that when I take time to edit and rethink what AI gives me, I understand my work better and feel more connected to it. When I rush and accept outputs quickly, it feels less meaningful. This shows that making is still about thinking and engagement, even when AI is involved.
