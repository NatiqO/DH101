# Reflection 5: How does AI alter authorship and remix culture?

## Response

AI changes authorship because creation is no longer done by just one person. The human still comes up with the idea and direction, but the AI helps generate and shape the final result. This shifts authorship from being about who made something entirely on their own to who guided the process and made the key decisions. Instead of being the sole creator, the person becomes more of a director who controls what the final outcome looks like.

This connects to *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots*, which explains that AI systems generate outputs by pulling from patterns in large datasets made up of existing human work. Because of this, AI is not creating something completely original, but remixing and recombining what already exists. This pushes remix culture further than before, since AI can blend styles, ideas, and content at a much larger scale and speed than humans alone.

This also raises questions about ownership. Since AI is trained on the work of many people, it becomes unclear who actually owns the final product. However, I think the person using the AI should be considered the owner because they are the one making the creative decisions, choosing prompts, and deciding what to keep or change. The AI does not have intention or responsibility, so it cannot truly be an author in the same way a human is.

## Personal Reflection

Personally, I see AI as something that expands creativity but also complicates it. It makes it easier to create, but it also makes it harder to define what counts as original. When I use AI, I feel like I am still the author because I decide how the final result looks. At the same time, I am aware that the output is influenced by many other sources, which makes authorship feel more shared than before.

## Source
