# Reflection 11: Who does the invisible work of AI, and who profits from it?

## Response

A lot of the work behind AI is done by people who are not visible, even though they play an important role in making these systems function. This includes data labelers, gig workers, content moderators, and even everyday users who generate data just by using apps and platforms. These groups help train and improve AI systems, but their contributions are often not recognized or fairly compensated.

This becomes clearer when looking at *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots*, which points out that large AI systems depend on massive datasets and resources that come from many different sources :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}. These datasets are built from human labor and activity, but the benefits are not distributed equally. Instead, major tech companies that develop and control these systems are the ones that profit the most.

Because of this, there is a clear imbalance between who does the work and who receives the rewards. The labor is spread across many people and regions, often in less visible or lower paid roles, while the profits are concentrated in a small number of powerful companies like Google and Microsoft. This makes AI not just a technical system, but also an economic and social one.

## Personal Reflection

Before learning about this, I mostly thought of AI as something created by engineers and developers. Now I realize that it depends on many different types of labor that are easy to overlook. It makes me think more critically about who is actually contributing to the technology I use and who is benefiting from it. It also shows that what seems like automated systems are still deeply connected to human work.
