# Reflection 8: Who is visible or invisible in AI networks?

## Response

When looking at AI networks, the most visible actors are usually large tech companies and well known researchers. They appear more central because they have more resources, more publications, and stronger institutional connections. As a result, they show up as the most influential nodes in network visualizations, which makes their power seem even more dominant.

A key idea from *Data Feminism* helps explain this by showing that data and visualizations are never neutral, but shaped by the choices of what is included and what is left out. In network maps, visibility is often based on measurable connections like citations or collaborations, which means that forms of labor that are harder to track are often ignored. This includes data workers, content moderators, and communities whose data is used but who are not recognized as contributors.

Because of this, visualization can both reveal and obscure power at the same time. It reveals patterns of influence among already powerful actors, but it can also hide the people and systems that make AI possible. By focusing on what is easy to measure, these visualizations can reinforce existing inequalities instead of challenging them.

## Personal Reflection

When I look at network visualizations now, I realize that they are not just showing reality, but shaping how we understand it. It is easy to assume that the biggest nodes are the most important, but that leaves out a lot of hidden work behind the scenes. This makes me more aware that what we do not see can be just as important as what we do.
