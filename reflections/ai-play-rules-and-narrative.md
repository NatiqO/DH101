# Reflection 10: How does AI change our relationship to play, rules, and narrative?

## Response

AI changes play by making it feel less like following a fixed set of rules and more like creating your own experience as you go. Instead of strict systems with clear outcomes, AI based systems can adjust in real time depending on what you do. This makes the experience feel smoother and more flexible, but also harder to fully understand because the rules are not always visible.

A key idea from *Critical Making in the Age of AI* is that digital systems often hide how they work, which makes it important to think critically about the tools we use :contentReference. In AI driven play, the system may feel open and creative, but it is still shaped by underlying structures and rules that are not always obvious. This means that even when something feels free or unpredictable, it is still being guided by design choices and programmed logic.

Narrative also changes because stories are no longer fixed. Instead of following one path, the story can shift based on your choices, making it feel more personal and interactive. This creates a stronger sense of involvement, since you are not just experiencing the story but helping shape it as it unfolds.

## Personal Reflection

When working with tools like Inky and AI systems, I noticed that I had some control, but the system was still deciding how things worked in the background. For example, it determines when something is a choice or a statement based on how it is written, even if it feels like I am fully in control. This made me realize that even interactive systems still rely on rules, they are just less visible. It feels more open, but there is still structure behind everything.
