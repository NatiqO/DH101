# Reflection 7: How does AI reshape global geographies of power, labor, and environment?

## Response

AI reshapes global geographies by concentrating power in a few tech hubs while distributing labor and environmental costs across other regions. Major AI companies and decision making centers are mostly located in places like the United States, which means control over AI systems and their development is concentrated in a small number of locations. At the same time, the work and resources needed to support AI are spread across the world.

This connects to *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots*, which highlights how large AI systems rely on massive datasets, energy use, and infrastructure. These systems depend on data centers, mining for materials, and human labor such as data labeling, which often takes place in other parts of the world. Because of this, the benefits of AI are not shared equally, while the costs are pushed onto different regions, especially those with fewer resources.

AI is also tied to global supply chains and resource extraction. Materials for hardware, energy for data centers, and outsourced digital labor all connect AI to environmental impact and labor conditions. This creates a system where power is centralized, but the negative effects are spread out.

## Personal Reflection

Personally, I did not think about how global AI systems really are until learning about this. It is easy to focus on the technology itself, but not where the resources and labor come from. This made me realize that AI is not just digital, but also physical and connected to real world systems. It also shows that the impacts of AI go beyond just users and affect people in different parts of the world in unequal ways.
