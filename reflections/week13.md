# Reflection 13: Do we imagine AI futures as utopian, dystopian, or something in between?

## Response

AI futures are not fully utopian or dystopian, but somewhere in between. It is unrealistic to think AI will solve every problem, but it is also unlikely to completely destroy society. More realistically, AI will amplify what already exists, improving certain aspects of life while also making existing problems more visible or even worse.

This becomes clearer when thinking about *Critical Making in the Age of AI*, which encourages people to actively question and engage with technology rather than accept it as neutral :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}. The way we imagine AI futures often reflects how we understand technology today. Utopian visions tend to focus on innovation and progress, while dystopian ones focus on control, inequality, and loss of agency. Both perspectives reveal more about human concerns and values than about the technology itself.

These visions show what people hope for and what they fear. Optimistic views highlight a desire for efficiency, creativity, and improvement, while negative views reflect concerns about power, control, and unintended consequences. Instead of being fixed outcomes, these futures depend on how people choose to design, use, and regulate AI systems.

## Personal Reflection

I tend to see AI as something in between because of how I have experienced it. It can be helpful and make things easier, but it also has clear limitations and risks. This made me realize that the future of AI is not just about the technology, but about the decisions people make around it. The way we imagine AI futures says more about what we want to change or protect in society than about what AI will actually become.
