# Reflection 6: What do we gain or lose when machines “read” literature for us?

## Response

When machines read literature for us, we gain speed, easier understanding, and quick access to ideas and themes. Tools like ChatGPT can explain meaning and summarize texts, while tools like Voyant can analyze patterns such as word frequency and structure. These tools can help break down complex texts and give insights that might take much longer to find on your own.

This connects to *Critical Making in the Age of AI*, which emphasizes the importance of actively engaging with tools rather than passively relying on them :contentReference. When machines read for us, they can support our understanding, but they also risk replacing the process of interpretation. Reading is not just about getting the “correct” meaning, but about thinking through the text and forming your own understanding.

At the same time, we lose important parts of the reading experience if we rely too much on machines. Personal interpretation, emotional connection, and deeper thinking can be reduced when answers are given too quickly. Machines can identify patterns and summarize ideas, but they cannot fully capture the meaning that comes from human perspective and experience.

## Personal Reflection

Personally, I have found tools like AI helpful for getting a general understanding of difficult texts, especially when I feel stuck. However, when I rely on them too much, I notice that I engage less with the text myself. It feels easier, but also less meaningful. This shows that while machines can support reading, they should not replace the process of thinking and interpreting on your own.
